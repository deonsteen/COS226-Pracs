Total workload was 120,000 jobs, bounded capacity 100, each row averaged over 5 runs.

The bounded queue was steady across all three workloads (about 2.8-3.1 million jobs/s). The lock-free queue was about the same as the bounded queue in the balanced and producer-heavy cases (4P/4C and 6P/2C). With 2 producers and 6 consumers it was much slower (1.8 million vs 2.8 million jobs/s).

The reason is the empty-queue spinning. The lock-free deq() returns null when the queue is empty, so idle consumers keep retrying. The empty-poll count was about 346,000 in the 2P/6C case, compared with about 8,000 in the producer-heavy case. Those spinning consumers waste CPU time and contend with the two producers. In the bounded queue, idle consumers sleep on the notEmpty condition and use no CPU.

With many producers (6P/2C) the lock-free queue never makes producers wait, since it is unbounded, but its advantage over the bounded queue was small in our results.

The lock-free queue is therefore not simply “faster”. Its performance depends on how the workload is balanced, and it does worst when consumers outnumber producers.
