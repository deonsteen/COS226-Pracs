Observations: The bounded queue was consistent across all three workloads (about 2.8-3.1 million jobs/s). The bounded queue was slightly faster in the balanced case (3.14M vs 2.72M jobs/s). In the producer-heavy case the two queues performed essentially the same (2.87M vs 2.92M).

The clearest difference is the consumer-heavy case (2P/6C), where lock-free fell to 1.8M jobs/s against 2.8M for the bounded queue. The likely cause is spinning. The lock-free deq() returns null when the queue is empty, so idle consumers retry in a loop. We counted about 346,000 empty polls in this case, against about 8,000 in the producer-heavy case. The six spinning consumers probably take CPU time and cache bandwidth away from the two producers. In the bounded queue, idle consumers wait on the notEmpty condition, which uses no CPU.

Conclusion: the lock-free queue is not simply faster. Its performance depends on the workload balance, and it did worst when consumers outnumbered producers. Each run took only about 0.04 s, so small differences (such as in the first two rows) may be noise, but the 2P/6C gap was large and consistent.
